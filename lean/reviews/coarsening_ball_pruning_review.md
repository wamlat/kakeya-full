# Independent review: Coarsening and BallPruning

Reviewed both current modules after the spatial assignment implementation. No false geometric, finite-count, scale or constant statement was found. No edits were made.

Coarsening assigns each physical fine-cell center to its unique actual half-open coarse grid cell. This definition does not assert that the whole fine cell lies inside that coarse cell; the later proofs use center assignment consistently. The center error is at most k*r/2, and original width*delta≤width*r correctly yields coarse admissibility width+k/2. Directions and bases remain unchanged.

The coarse fiber bound is genuinely a tube bound of order r/delta. It encloses one coarse cell inside a ball of radius(k+1)r, then applies the independently proved local tube-grid count. The extra dimension factor and width factors are included in fiberConstant. Exact partition of the fine set by the actual coarse labels then gives the lower coarse count |S|*delta/(fiberConstant*r). Empty sets and zero-dimensional formal edge cases are harmless. The heavy-cell mass inequality uses disjoint actual fibers, so it has no hidden overlap factor or multiplicity error.

BallPruning constructs, rather than assumes, a bounded number of coarse cells covering any fine-center ball. The image coarse centers lie within rho+k*r/2 of the actual ball center, and the proved integer-grid ball count supplies coverConstant(k,R)=5^k*(1+R+k/2)^k whenever rho≤Rr. The fiber count is applied to the actual ball subset and summed over its occupied coarse labels.

The chosen dyadic scale in the small-radius argument lies between rho and2rho, including the case where the lowest tested dyadic scale is slightly larger than rho. The real exponent d is explicitly nonnegative for the real-power monotonicity step. The survivor definition checks the ORIGINAL set's coarse-fiber population at every tested scale; thus every original cell belonging to any originally heavy fiber is removed. Choosing one survivor in a nonempty surviving fiber correctly transfers its original nonheavy bound to the whole surviving fiber. Empty surviving fibers are treated separately.

For radii above1, the proof bounds total surviving population at unit coarse scale by covering the prescribed fixed radius-R region with actual coarse grid cells. It then uses d≥0 and rho≥1 to extend the unit-scale estimate. The final coefficient max(coverConstant(k,1)*2^d,coverConstant(k,R))*F is independent of delta, actual cap center and tested radius. This supplies the all-radii domain needed by LiftGraph, including its cap-induced label balls whose radius can exceed1. The bounded-region radius R must remain fixed before scale variation, as the statement makes explicit.

A critical scope boundary remains: neither module proves a lower bound on the size or incidence mass of the survivor set. It can be empty, for example when F=0 and the original set is nonempty. The all-ball estimate is valid in that case, but does not by itself establish the manuscript's quantitative pruning retention. Combining the heavy-cell deletion with the analytic induction/cutoff argument to bound deleted mass remains a separate obligation. This is not an error in these theorem statements; it is essential to avoid presenting the constructed cap bound alone as the full analytic pruning step.
