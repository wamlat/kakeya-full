A cell in two distinct group unions would therefore have two unequal values of the same global label function. This proves disjointness of the *old label sets*, not just disjointness of boxes or a volume estimate. The finite union identity then yields `sum #groupUnion ≤ #originalUnion`. Multiple tubes in one bin may share cells; those cells are counted once in that bin's union. Every original tube belongs to exactly one group, so group populations sum exactly to `M`.

Using one retained point bounds each associated original localization center within `(1+n)rho` of this origin. The argument uses `delta ≤ rho` only where stated and imposes no divisibility or integral scale-ratio condition. Inside a bin, all tubes use the same spatial dilation and the same integer translation `z ↦ z−origin`; this translation is injective. Tube-dependent parameters `a_i` choose actual short axis intervals, but do not change the shading-label map. Consequently both individual row cardinalities and the entire group-union cardinality are preserved exactly.

Selected shades remain admissible. Relative two ends transfers to the selected rows with coefficient `2K*B` by subset counting and the proved proportional retention. The actual short-interval construction supplies unit rescaled axes, admissibility at `delta/(8W*rho)`, and the base bound `(6W+1+n)/(8W)`, where `W=max(1,width)`.

The report's limitations are accurate. The construction starts from actual radius-`rho` localization balls and factor-two comparable original rows; it does not itself establish spatial localization for an arbitrary family. `normalized_group` supplies a genuine bounded unit-tube family, but does not yet assert normalized density at most one, target-scale direction separation, a normalized absolute cap coefficient, or a final analytic estimate. Those belong to the subsequent larger-dilation/thinning and two-ends application. Separate groups may have different origins; the justified summation remains over their disjoint old cell sets using each group's exact cardinality identity, and requires no claim that the separately rescaled sets are mutually disjoint.
